# The report after a note

What the player (or parent) reads and hears after any practice, match, lesson or tournament note. The app first sends an instant acknowledgement; this report follows when the team's work is done. It is the most important thing the team writes. The reader should finish it thinking "yes, that's it, I'd forgotten that, and here's what I do next."

## Before writing

1. **Read their words closely.** Long dictations are messy. Repair tennis mishearings ("bow"/"boat" = ball, "hi bro" = high ball, "surf" = serve) and keep their best phrases verbatim. Their own words beat any form field.
2. **Health comes only from their words.** Mention the body only if they described pain, injury or limping in this note. "A pain in the ass", "nothing hurts" and "no limping" are not pain. Never write "no pain in this note" and never open with a health line unless they reported a problem.
3. **Find the pattern they missed.** Search, in order:
   - `journal/patterns.md`;
   - the last 10 entries in `journal/entries.md`;
   - `journal/history/` (grep the shot, opponent or feeling);
   - the coach's `principles.md` and `study-notes.md`.

   Look for two or three problems that are really ONE cause, a date when the same thing happened, a date when the fix worked, or a coach teaching that explains the miss. It must be true and cite a date or source. With no history yet, use the note itself (two things in it that share a cause), and never say "this is your first session".
4. **Pick ONE fix, and make it the fix for that pattern.** The fix must address the cause you named. Never name a problem as solved in one part and as the fix in another.
5. **Progress.** If the same thing was counted or described before, compare it in one line ("returns: 6 of 10 in play, up from 4 on Oct 12"). Skip it when there's nothing to compare.
6. **The coach's voice.** Read `coaches/<slug>/voice.md` (or `references/house-voice.md`). Explain the way that coach does: start from the miss they know, give the body chain, one image, a two-beat cue. Build the drill the coach's way (their progression, e.g. shadow → toss and catch → into the box → count; "misses don't matter at first").
7. **Fit their real setting.** Use only what the player has: who they hit with, clinic vs. private, practice length (profile, the note). A drill needing big serves from a partner who doesn't have one is useless. Give no homework they didn't ask for: fitness or extra sessions appear only if the plan changed.

8. **Facts exactly as given.** Scores, counts, order and who-did-what are restated exactly as the note (or the journal entry) says. Never merge two notes into one claim ("two in a row" when it was one at 5-5 and one at 6-6), and never round a count. If you compare with an earlier note, state each fact separately.
9. **Only words they'd recognise:** their own phrases, or their coach's cue words. Don't invent labels ("your coil-then-legs idea"), and don't bring up past ideas this note didn't mention unless they're the evidence for the pattern.

## Player report (adult or junior player)

**What I heard.** Two or three short bullets in their words (key phrases in quotes). For a short note, one line.

**What you might have missed.** Say the pattern in one plain sentence first ("It's one problem: your legs."). Then at most two pieces of evidence, each clearly the same cause, with dates.

**Working.** One line, plus the progress line if there is one.

**The one fix.** In the coach's voice:
- the miss as they know it;
- the body chain ("legs stop driving → arm takes over → the ball flies");
- one image;
- ONE cue in italics (two beats if the coach has one; never two cues).

For a recreational player without deep technique (no real coach, or a house voice), lead with the image and keep the body chain to two links.

Three to five sentences. Technique only from the player's coach (rule 20); if the coach hasn't covered it, say so and make it a question for the next lesson.

**Next session.** The coach-style progression in two or three steps, then what to count and the target, and when in the session. It must fit their setting, and it must be something they'd do at the club. Use shadow or no-ball steps only if they practise alone or their coach uses them.

**Plan.** One line, only about what this note changes or confirms. Don't list unrelated priorities, check-ins or reminders.

Length is a hard limit: at most **200 words** (about 130 for a short note). Count before you finish. If you're over, cut in this order: "What I heard" down to two bullets, the evidence in "What you might have missed" down to two dated examples, then the image. Never lead with meta ("this is the same note…").

## Parent report (mode=parent)

The parent isn't the coach. Write for them, about the junior by name, and never write a phrase that would hurt if the junior read it or the parent repeated it ("lost two points without a rally" → "the tiebreak got away on two serves").

**What happened.** Two short, kind, factual bullets.

**What's going on.** The one pattern in plain words (e.g. "when it matters, he speeds up"), with a date if the history shows it before.

**In the car (or tonight).**
- **Say:** one or two short lines in quotes, in the way a parent really talks to a teen (no coaching-speech, nothing that sounds scripted), about effort or a specific good moment.
- **Don't bring up:** the thing to leave alone today.
- **If he's quiet:** what to do (usually: let him be; offer food or music; talk tomorrow).

**For Coach [name].** A copy-ready message of one or two sentences, phrased as an observation and a question, never an instruction and never a judgement of the coach's advice ("he still…"). Facts in it must be exact. For example: "Alex skipped the serve routine in the tiebreak today. Is there a way to make it stick under pressure?"

**Your part vs. the coach's.** One line (any reminder gets a date): what the parent can do (a routine at home, rest, food, the calendar) and what's the coach's (technique, drills at the academy). Give no drills for practices the coach runs. A home or solo routine is fine if the junior practises alone.

Length is a hard limit: at most **180 words**. Count before you finish.

## The spoken version (`say`)

30-45 seconds (about 80-110 words). Not a replay of the written report:
- **Player:** the pattern in one line, the fix with its cue, the next-session drill.
- **Parent:** what's going on, then what to say in the car.

Numbers as words, no lists, end on the action. Never a question.

## Rules

- **No questions** unless TEAM.md rule 8 truly requires one. "Next time, notice whether…" instead. An unclear word gets one line: "I read 'hi bro' as high ball."
- **No filing talk:** never say which teammate saved what, and no IDs or file names. Crediting the coach is fine.
- **No invented facts.** Every claim comes from their words, their journal, or their coach's material.
