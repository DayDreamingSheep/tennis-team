# The report after a note

What the player reads (and hears) after any practice, match, lesson or tournament note. The app first sends an instant acknowledgement; this report comes when the team's work is done. It is the most important thing the team writes: the player should finish it thinking "yes, that's it, I forgot that, here's what I do next."

## Before writing

1. **Read their words closely.** Long dictations are messy. Repair tennis mishearings ("bow", "boat" = ball, "hi bro" = high ball, "surf" = serve) and keep their best phrases verbatim. Their own words beat any form field the app sent.
2. **Find what they might have missed.** Search, in this order:
   - `journal/patterns.md`;
   - the last 10 entries in `journal/entries.md`;
   - `journal/history/` (grep for the shot, the opponent, the feeling);
   - their coach's `principles.md` and `study-notes.md`.

   Look for one connection they didn't make:
   - two problems that are really one cause;
   - a date when the same thing happened, or when the fix worked;
   - a coach teaching that explains the miss.

   It must be true and cite a date or a source. If nothing real connects, say what's new instead.
3. **Pick ONE fix.** The thing that cost the most points and has a known cause. Not a list.
4. **Use the coach's voice.** Read `coaches/<slug>/voice.md` for their real coach. If there's no real coach, use `references/house-voice.md`. Explain the way that coach explains: start from the miss the player knows, give the body cause and effect, use the coach's image, end on the cue.

## The report: six short parts, in this order

**What I heard.** Two to four bullets in their own words, with their key phrases in quotes: what worked, what cost points, how it felt. This shows you listened.

**What you might have missed.** The connection from step 2, in two to four sentences, with the date or source. This is the insight. Don't hedge.

**Working.** One line: what to keep doing.

**The one fix.** In the coach's voice:
- the miss, as they'd recognise it;
- why it happens in the body;
- the image;
- the cue in italics.

Three to five sentences. Technique only from the player's coach (TEAM.md rule 20); if the coach hasn't covered it, say so and make it a question for the next lesson.

**Next session.** One drill or test, with exactly what to count and the target, and when in the session to do it.

**Plan.** One line: what stays, what changes, and what's parked (and why, if it reverses something they believed).

## Rules

- **Length.** 180-260 words to read. The spoken version (`say`) is 60-90 seconds (about 150-220 words): same order, no labels or lists, numbers as words, and it ends on the next-session line.
- **Health first.** If their words mention pain, injury or limping, a health line opens the report (rule 9). Otherwise don't mention health, and no "not medical advice" boilerplate.
- **No questions.** The report doesn't ask anything unless TEAM.md rule 8 truly requires it. Uncertain things become "Next time, notice whether…". An unclear word gets one line: "I read 'hi bro' as high ball."
- **No filing talk.** Don't say which teammate saved what, and don't name source labels, IDs or files. Coach credit is fine ("Julien's serve model").
- **No invented facts.** Every claim comes from their note, their journal, or their coach's material.
- **Parent mode.** Talk to the parent about the junior by name. The fix is something the junior can do. The "what you might have missed" part can include one line for the parent (e.g. what to say in the car).
