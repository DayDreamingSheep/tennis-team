# Evidence rules

## Authority order (highest first)

1. `DIRECT` - coaching given specifically to this player: the player's write-up of a lesson, the coach's written feedback, or feedback on the player's video. When the write-up paraphrases rather than quotes, use confidence `medium` unless the wording is specific and unambiguous.
2. `PLAYER` - the player's own ideas or interpretations that cite the coach ("I think this is what Maria means").
3. `PUB` - the coach's official public material (videos, articles, books, courses).
4. General tennis knowledge.

Lower levels may fill gaps but never silently override a higher level. If levels conflict, show the conflict.

## Verdicts

- `aligned` - matches relevant evidence without meaningful conflict.
- `partly aligned` - the core idea matches, but scope, context, timing, or wording needs correction.
- `likely conflict` - relevant evidence points against the claim.
- `insufficient evidence` - the stored record cannot support a coach-specific conclusion.

## Confidence

- `high` - direct, specific, context-matched, no material contradiction.
- `medium` - relevant but indirect, incomplete, or context-dependent (e.g. stated for backhand, applied to forehand).
- `low` - tentative, weakly matched, or contradicted.

## Principle vs hypothesis

Promote to a principle when at least one holds:
- a `DIRECT` source states it specifically for that scope; or
- two or more independent `PUB` sources state it consistently.

Keep as a hypothesis when it is an inference, a transfer to a new stroke, a sensation-based interpretation, or a single public mention. Every hypothesis needs a validation test (what would confirm or reject it in practice).

Demote a principle back to hypothesis, or mark it scoped, when later direct evidence contradicts it. Never delete history; note the change with a date.

## Sensation cues

Feel-based cues ("slap", "arm-wrestling", "figure eight") are only useful while they preserve relaxation, timing, and contact. Flag any cue that is being forced into a position.
